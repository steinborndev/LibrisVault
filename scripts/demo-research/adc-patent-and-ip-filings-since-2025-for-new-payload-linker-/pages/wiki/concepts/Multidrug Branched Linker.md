# Multidrug Branched Linker

A linker architecture with a single antibody-coupling group feeding into two or three separate drug-attachment arms, so one conjugation reaction installs multiple payload molecules through one linker molecule - as opposed to running two independent conjugation chemistries at two separate sites, the approach used by [[Dual-Payload Antibody-Drug Conjugate]]'s two-linker filings.

## Why it matters

Conventional single-payload ADCs are already pushed toward high drug-to-antibody ratios (DAR) to increase potency, but stacking hydrophobic cytotoxic payloads onto an antibody tends to cause aggregation - the conjugate clumps in solution, which is both a manufacturing problem (process scale-up) and a safety one (aggregates clear faster and can be more immunogenic). A branched multidrug linker concentrates that hydrophobicity problem in one purpose-built structure, which is easier to fix than fixing it separately.

## Detail

[[Multidrug Linker and Antibody-Drug Conjugate (patent)]] (Shanghai Huao Co Ltd, WO2025031307A1, priority 2023-08-04, pending) claims this architecture explicitly: a coupling group (Q) for antibody attachment, variable spacer units, peptide or glucose connectors, and two or three drug-attachment spacers (T1-T3), with the claim requiring that at least one of the branch or spacer positions carry a hydrophilic substituent - polysarcosine, polyethylene glycol, or a cyclodextrin fragment are named. The hydrophilic substitution is the actual novelty claimed, not the branching itself: the patent frames branching-without-hydrophilic-compensation as the prior-art failure mode that causes aggregation and precipitation.

This sits inside a broader trend the [[ADC Linker Chemistry Technology Landscape 2026 (industry report)]] identifies across the wider (single-payload) linker literature too: hydrophilic linker design, historically a differentiator (Daiichi Sankyo's earlier hydrophilic-linker filings, 2014-2018), is becoming a baseline expectation rather than a novel claim on its own - the report notes CSPC Megalith's 2025 AU filings and Otsuka's 2025 IN/EP filings (linkers bearing COOH, SO3H, or PO3H2 groups) both lead with hydrophilicity as the primary claim, and Shanghai Huao's multidrug-linker filing extends the same fix to the harder, multi-payload case.

## In practice

- A branched multidrug linker and two independent single-payload linkers achieve a similar outcome (two payloads, one antibody) through different claim scopes - check which architecture a given filing actually claims before treating them as equivalent prior art against each other.
- Hydrophilic substitution language ("at least one of [position] is substituted with a hydrophilic group") is the search term worth grepping for across this class of filing; it is where 2025's actual novelty concentrates, not in the coupling chemistry.
