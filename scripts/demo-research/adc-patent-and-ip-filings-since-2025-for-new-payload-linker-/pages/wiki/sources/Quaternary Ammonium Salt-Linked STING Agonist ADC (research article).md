# Quaternary Ammonium Salt-Linked STING Agonist ADC (research article)

"Novel Quaternary Ammonium Salt-Linked STING Agonist Antibody-Drug Conjugate: Synergistic Activation of Tumor Immunity with Mitigated Off-Target Toxicity," Long et al., *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 2025. Peer-reviewed preclinical research, not a patent filing - included as the clearest publicly-reachable description of a 2025 STING-agonist-payload ADC advance, as background for the payload-class trend this pass's synthesis covers.

## Summary

Reports a STING agonist payload conjugated via a quaternary-ammonium-salt linkage, claimed to give high potency, improved chemical/plasma stability, enhanced aqueous solubility, and reduced off-target toxicity relative to prior ester-linked STING-agonist ADC approaches. Frames the advance explicitly against the class's known failure mode: XMT-2056 (STING agonist XMT-1616 conjugated to trastuzumab via a cleavable ester linker) showed strong preclinical activity but an unstable linker that contributed to severe adverse events and patient deaths in a Phase I trial - a linker-stability failure rather than a payload-mechanism failure. The quaternary-ammonium-salt chemistry is presented as a fix targeted specifically at that stability problem.

- Supports [[STING Agonist Payload (ADC)]]: primary evidence for the payload class's 2025 state of the art, and for linker stability (not the STING mechanism itself) being the specific engineering problem 2025 work targets.

## Caveats

- A peer-reviewed research paper, not a patent filing - no claim scope, priority date, or IP ownership position is established by this source; whether a patent filing covers this specific linkage chemistry was not checked in this pass.
- Preclinical only; no clinical data for this specific construct was found in this pass.
- The XMT-2056 clinical-death account is reported here as background context from the same literature, not independently verified against a primary clinical-trial report in this pass.
