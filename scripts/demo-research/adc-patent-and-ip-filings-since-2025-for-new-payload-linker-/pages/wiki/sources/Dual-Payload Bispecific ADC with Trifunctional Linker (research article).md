# Dual-Payload Bispecific ADC with Trifunctional Linker (research article)

"A Dual-Payload Bispecific ADC Improved Potency and Efficacy over Single-Payload Bispecific ADCs," published in *Pharmaceutics* (MDPI), August 2025 (PMID 40870990). Preclinical research, funded by Tavotek Biotherapeutics - a peer-reviewed journal article, not a patent filing, included here as the clearest publicly-reachable description of a dual-payload-plus-bispecific construct actually built and tested, as background for the patent filings covered elsewhere in this pass.

## Summary

Builds a dual-targeting, dual-payload ADC: a bispecific EGFR x cMET antibody carrying two payloads with different mechanisms of action, attached via one trifunctional linker core bearing maleimide, propargyl, and ketone handles. MMAF (a tubulin-polymerisation inhibitor) is attached via oxime ligation through a non-cleavable PEG4-aminooxy arm; SN-38 (a topoisomerase I inhibitor, delivered as PEG8-Val-Cit-PABC-SN38) is attached via copper-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition through a cleavable arm - two payloads, two independent release mechanisms, one linker molecule. Reports that the dual-payload bispecific construct inhibited cell proliferation more effectively than single-payload comparators across multiple cancer cell lines, and produced more potent in vivo tumor growth inhibition than single-payload bispecific ADCs.

## Extracted claims

- Supports [[Dual-Payload Antibody-Drug Conjugate]]: a built-and-tested (not just claimed) example of the trifunctional-linker route to dual payloads, distinct from both the two-independent-linkers and branched-linker patent architectures found elsewhere in this pass.
- Supports [[Bispecific Antibody-Drug Conjugate]]: direct preclinical evidence that combining the dual-target and dual-payload axes outperforms either axis alone, in this specific EGFR x cMET / MMAF+SN-38 system.

## Caveats

- A preclinical research paper, not a patent - no claim scope, priority date, or filing status attaches to it; it demonstrates feasibility, not IP ownership over the construct.
- Funded by Tavotek Biotherapeutics; no separate Tavotek patent filing covering this specific construct was found or verified in this pass, so the IP position (if any) behind this published result is unknown.
- Single-study result (one paper, one set of cell lines and one in vivo model) - not independently replicated as of this pass.
