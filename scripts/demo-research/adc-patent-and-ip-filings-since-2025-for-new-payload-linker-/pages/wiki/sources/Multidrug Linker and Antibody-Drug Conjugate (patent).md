# Multidrug Linker and Antibody-Drug Conjugate (patent)

WO2025031307A1, applicant Shanghai Huao Co Ltd. Priority date 2023-08-04, filed 2024-08-05, published 2025-02-13. Status: pending (no grant found in this pass). Read via the Google Patents record, not the original PDF.

## Summary

Claims a branched linker (Formulas I-III) for attaching two or three distinct drug molecules to a single antibody through one coupling event: a coupling group (Q) for antibody attachment, variable spacer units (C1, B1, B2), peptide or glucose connector units (P1-P3), and drug-attachment spacers (T1-T3). The dependent claims require that at least one of B2, T1, T2 or T3 be substituted with a hydrophilic group - polysarcosine, polyethylene glycol, or a cyclodextrin fragment are all named as options.

The stated problem is aggregation: branching multiple hydrophobic cytotoxic payloads off one linker tends to make the resulting conjugate precipitate or aggregate in solution, which both complicates manufacturing scale-up and can degrade product quality. The hydrophilic substitution is claimed as the fix, said to reduce aggregation while preserving activity against drug-resistant tumors.

## Extracted claims

- Supports [[Multidrug Branched Linker]]: the primary evidence for the branched-linker (vs. two-independent-linkers) architecture, and for hydrophilic substitution as the specific claimed fix to the aggregation problem that architecture creates.
