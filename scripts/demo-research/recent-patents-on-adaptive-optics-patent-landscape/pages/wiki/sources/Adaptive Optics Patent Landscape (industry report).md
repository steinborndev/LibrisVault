# Adaptive Optics Patent Landscape (industry report)

A patent-analytics vendor's blog post, "Adaptive Optics Patent Landscape 2026" (Patsnap R&D blog), summarising a database query over patent families classified under adaptive optics. Not a primary patent document - a secondary aggregation over many of them - and not peer reviewed; read as a directional map of where filing activity sits, not as an authoritative count.

## Summary

Scope: 4,083 patent families. Top assignees by record count: Texas Instruments (652, concentrated in optical beam-steering and spatial light modulation), Magic Leap (447, augmented-reality optics), Raytheon (397), Hamamatsu Photonics (261), Olympus (252) - the top five hold 27% of the top hundred filers' combined total. Annual filings peaked in 2020 at 600 records, up from 521 in 2017, and have declined since (a lag-adjusted -39% over three years); the report notes the 2024-2025 figures undercount true filing volume because publication lags filing. By IPC classification, G02B (optical elements and systems) dominates with 11,261 records, well ahead of H01S (lasers, 1,638), G02F (optical control/modulation, 1,601) and H04N (pictorial communication, 1,596). The United States leads by jurisdiction; China is rising, with the Institute of Optics and Electronics (Chinese Academy of Sciences) showing +17% recent momentum.

The headline finding for this vault: patent volume in "adaptive optics" as a classification is dominated by consumer AR/VR optics (Texas Instruments, Magic Leap) and defense (Raytheon), not by astronomical instrumentation. Astronomy is a downstream beneficiary of sensor and beam-steering IP developed for other markets, rather than the field driving the filings.

## Caveats

- Vendor content marketing a patent-analytics product; figures are self-reported from Patsnap's own database and were not cross-checked against USPTO/EPO/CNIPA totals directly.
- "Adaptive optics" as a classification bucket is broad and includes consumer optics, ophthalmology, and laser processing alongside astronomical instrumentation; the assignee ranking reflects that breadth, not astronomy specifically.
- 2024-2025 counts are explicitly flagged by the source itself as undercounts due to publication lag.
