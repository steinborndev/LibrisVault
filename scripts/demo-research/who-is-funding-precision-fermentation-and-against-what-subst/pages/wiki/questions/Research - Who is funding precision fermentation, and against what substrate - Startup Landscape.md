# Research - Who is funding precision fermentation, and against what substrate - Startup Landscape

## Question

Who is putting capital into precision-fermentation startups right now, how much, and - the
part press coverage usually buries - what is the host organism actually being fed? Substrate
choice turns out to be the variable that best predicts a company's cost structure, risk
profile, and (per one funded, now-insolvent example) survival.

## Method

Searched 2026 trade press (AgFunderNews, Green Queen, vegconomist, EU-Startups, Food
Navigator, World Bio Market Insights) and one investor's own published framework (Synthesis
Capital) for named funding rounds, investor syndicates, and stated feedstocks. Prioritized
articles reporting a specific round with named investors and dollar figures over
market-size/CAGR projections, which are abundant but say nothing about who is actually
writing checks. Cross-checked the sector-wide funding trend against the Good Food Institute's
2026 State of the Industry (Fermentation) figures, read via a secondary AgFunderNews summary
rather than the primary GFI PDF - the PDF itself is queued on the reading list rather than
fetched in this run (see [[Reading list]]). Followed the "Startups & funding" lens: emphasis
on company-level rounds, backers and traction, not on the underlying microbiology.

## Findings

### 1. Overall funding is down sharply, but precision fermentation specifically still drew capital in 2025-2026

Fermentation-derived alt-protein funding fell from $632 million in 2024 to $357 million in
2025 (a 43.5% decline), per GFI, attributed to the field maturing past early hype toward
harder proof of commercial viability - sharpened by visible failures including Meati, Motif
FoodWorks and [[Arkeon]] ([[GFI Fermentation Funding Dip 2025 (article)]]). Within that
smaller pool, precision fermentation specifically (as opposed to biomass or solid-state
fermentation) still closed several eight-figure rounds in the period covered by this run:
[[The Every Company]] ($55M, egg protein), [[Standing Ovation]] ($34.2M, casein), Vivici
($34M, whey/lactoferrin, not covered in depth in this pass), and [[Verley]] ($38M, whey BLG).
[[Perfect Day]], the sector's longest-running and most heavily capitalized company, closed a
smaller pre-Series E (up to $90M) against a cumulative total past $825M - one investor's
public framing was that these later, smaller, existing-investor-led rounds mark a sector
"crossing the line from promise to proof," but that framing is contestable: it is equally
consistent with a sector where new investors have grown more cautious.

### 2. The investor syndicate composition differs sharply by geography and stage, and government money is now a lead investor, not a bystander

US rounds ([[The Every Company]], [[Perfect Day]]) are led by specialist food-tech VC funds
and existing investors. French rounds ([[Standing Ovation]], [[Verley]]) both include
Bpifrance, France's state investment bank, as either co-lead or a material non-dilutive
contributor under the France 2030 industrial-policy programme - in Standing Ovation's case,
Bpifrance's Ecotechnologies 2 fund co-led ahead of, not alongside, the specialist and
corporate-strategic investors. Corporate strategics also recur: Bel Group (cheese) backed
Standing Ovation, Danone Ventures backed Standing Ovation, and Ajinomoto Group Ventures - an
established industrial fermentation producer - backed [[Fermeate]]. This suggests corporate
and sovereign capital, not generalist VC, is increasingly what closes precision-fermentation
rounds, a pattern this run did not find discussed explicitly in any single source but that
emerges from comparing the syndicates directly.

### 3. Substrate choice sorts funded companies into a low-risk and a high-risk tier, and investors appear to price that difference

Feedstock (media) accounts for 35-50% of cost of goods sold in a typical precision
fermentation process, with downstream purification sometimes larger still (up to 85% of cost
for some biologics) ([[Precision Fermentation Pathways to Cost Parity (insight)]]). Against
that backdrop, funded companies split cleanly by substrate:

- **Sugar and dairy/food-industry side-streams (lower risk, most funded rounds)**:
  [[Standing Ovation]] feeds its host acid whey (a cottage-cheese/yogurt byproduct)
  supplemented with sugar, explicitly to avoid "buy[ing] food to build food"; Hyfe
  valorizes brewery and cannery wastewater into feed
  ([[Most Sustainable Feedstocks for Precision Fermentation (article)]]). [[Verley]] uses a
  fungal host but did not disclose its feedstock.
- **C1 substrates (methanol) with enabling-technology plays on top**: methylotrophic hosts
  like *Pichia pastoris* run on methanol; [[Fermeate]]'s optogenetic retrofit is pitched
  partly as a way to swap the methanol chemical inducer for light control, changing
  substrate-handling cost rather than the substrate itself.
- **Gas fermentation - CO2, hydrogen, methane (higher risk, smaller and rarer rounds)**:
  [[Solar Foods]] (CO2 + electrolytic hydrogen, ~$38M raised across ten rounds plus a EUR10M
  government grant) and [[Arkeon]] (CO2 + hydrogen, just over $13M raised) sit in this tier.
  Arkeon's founder maintains the economics could eventually match incumbent sugar-fed amino
  acid production at scale, but the company filed for insolvency in 2026 after a funding round
  collapsed when a lead investor withdrew - and he attributes part of that fragility to
  investors treating gas fermentation as carrying "definitely higher" risk than sugar- or
  side-stream-fed processes, independent of the underlying unit-economics case
  ([[Arkeon Postmortem (article)]]).

No source in this run reported a company running commercial-scale precision fermentation on
raw, unprocessed lignocellulosic (forestry) waste yet; Fibenol's pretreated C5/C6 sugars
(supplying [[Fermentation Feedstock Economics]]'s downstream users, e.g. ÄIO) is the closest
example found, and it still requires a hydrolysis step before a fermentation host can use it.

## What this changes about the vault

This is new subject matter for the wiki: no existing page substantively covered industrial
precision fermentation as biotechnology or its investment landscape (the vault's existing
"Fermentation"/"Precision" pages - [[Sourdough Fermentation]], [[Bulk Fermentation]],
[[Precision and Recall]], [[Photometric Precision]] - are about culinary technique, ML
evaluation, and astrophysics respectively, and share only vocabulary with this topic, not
subject matter). Added two concepts ([[Precision Fermentation]],
[[Fermentation Feedstock Economics]]) and seven entities meeting the notability bar as subjects of dedicated,
fact-rich sources ([[Standing Ovation]], [[Verley]], [[Fermeate]], [[Perfect Day]],
[[The Every Company]], [[Solar Foods]], [[Arkeon]]). Investors (Bpifrance, Synthesis Capital,
Ajinomoto Group Ventures, McWin Capital Partners, etc.) were deliberately kept as inline
attribution on the relevant entity/source pages rather than given their own entity pages, per
this run's notability rule: each appears only as a participant in a funding round, not as the
subject of a source.

## Open questions

- Whether the syndicate pattern in finding 2 (state/sovereign capital co-leading French
  rounds, specialist VC leading US rounds) holds beyond the four rounds compared here, or is
  an artifact of which two countries' press this run happened to search most.
- Whether Verley's undisclosed feedstock is sugar, a dairy side-stream, or something else -
  no source found in this run states it directly.
- Whether the "gas fermentation carries higher investor-perceived risk" pattern (Arkeon,
  and Solar Foods' reliance on a government grant rather than a full commercial equity round)
  generalizes, or whether Solar Foods' larger cumulative raise and grant support show that
  risk can be underwritten by non-dilutive government capital specifically for CO2-based
  approaches.
- The primary GFI 2026 State of the Industry (Fermentation) PDF was not read directly in this
  run (only a secondary summary); the sector-wide funding figures above should be confirmed
  against it before being treated as final. Queued on [[Reading list]].
