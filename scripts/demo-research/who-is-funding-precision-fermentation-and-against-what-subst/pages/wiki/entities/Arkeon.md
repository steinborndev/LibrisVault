# Arkeon

Vienna-based gas-fermentation startup ("protein from air") that converted CO2 and hydrogen
into amino acids, using bacteria descended from ancient CO2-metabolising lineages. Filed for
insolvency in 2026, making it one of the sector's clearest funded-but-failed case studies.

## Funding

Raised just over $13 million across rounds, including an oversubscribed seed of more than
$7 million from Square One Foods, Synthesis Capital and ReGen Ventures, later joined by ICL
and aws Gründungsfonds. A follow-on raise begun successfully in 2024 collapsed after a major
committed investor withdrew unexpectedly, which caused other prospective investors to pull
back as well ([[Arkeon Postmortem (article)]]).

## Substrate

CO2 and hydrogen. The company initially chose grey hydrogen (derived from natural gas rather
than electrolysis) for cost and availability reasons in Europe, illustrating that even a
"low-carbon" gas-fermentation pitch can run on a fossil-derived input at the margin - see
[[Fermentation Feedstock Economics]].

## Why it failed

CEO Gregor Tegl attributed the shutdown to timing rather than to the underlying technology:
extended regulatory and development timelines, a strategic pivot from mixed amino acids to a
single amino acid that disrupted existing industry-partner agreements, and anticipated deals
that "didn't materialize in time." He maintained the gas-fermentation process could "match
existing amino acid production processes on price at scale," but acknowledged investors treat
the category as carrying "definitely higher" risk than sugar- or side-stream-fed fermentation.
