# Precision Fermentation

Precision fermentation uses an engineered host microorganism (a yeast, fungus, or bacterium)
to produce one specific target molecule - usually a protein such as a milk or egg protein, an
enzyme, or a fat - rather than harvesting the microbial biomass itself. This distinguishes it
from **biomass fermentation** (where the microbial cells themselves are the product, as with
mycoprotein) and traditional/solid-state fermentation (used to transform a food matrix, e.g.
tempeh or miso). The GFI's fermentation State of the Industry reporting treats these three as
distinct subsectors of the same broader field, and funding trends differ across them (see
[[GFI Fermentation Funding Dip 2025 (article)]]).

Not to be confused with the culinary sense of "fermentation" used elsewhere in this wiki
([[Sourdough Fermentation]], [[Bulk Fermentation]]) - those pages describe baking technique
in the cooking domain and share no subject matter with the industrial biotechnology use
covered here beyond the word itself.

## Why it matters (for funding)

The economics of precision fermentation, not the biology, are what currently gate investment.
A host organism is engineered once; after that, unit economics are set by feedstock cost,
facility depreciation, and downstream purification - see [[Fermentation Feedstock Economics]]
for the cost breakdown. Investors increasingly price rounds against progress on those levers
rather than against novelty of the target molecule.

## What gets funded, by product

- **Dairy proteins** - casein ([[Standing Ovation]]), whey/beta-lactoglobulin ([[Verley]],
  [[Perfect Day]], Vivici), lactoferrin (All G).
- **Egg proteins** - [[The Every Company]] (ovalbumin and related egg proteins).
- **Single-cell protein from gas feedstocks** - [[Solar Foods]] (CO2 + hydrogen), Calysta and
  LanzaTech (methane and waste industrial gases), [[Arkeon]] (CO2 + hydrogen, insolvent 2026).
- **Enabling technology** - [[Fermeate]] sells a retrofit (optogenetic strain control) that
  raises yield on existing fermentation infrastructure rather than making a protein itself.

## In practice

- A precision-fermentation pitch is, in practice, a feedstock-and-downstream-processing
  pitch: check what the organism is fed and how the product is purified before comparing two
  companies' claimed timelines to price parity.
- "Precision fermentation" in press coverage is sometimes used loosely for any microbial
  protein production; check whether the source actually means a single defined molecule
  (precision) or bulk biomass before treating two articles as comparable.
- Gas-fermentation pitches (CO2, methane, hydrogen) carry a materially higher stated risk
  profile than sugar- or dairy-side-stream-fed pitches - see [[Arkeon]] for a funded example
  that ran out of runway.
